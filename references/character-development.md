# Narrative Character Development

Load this file at **Stage 1** alongside `references/character-design.md` (visual silhouette) and `templates/character-profile.md` so every cast member reads like a believable person rather than a plot tool.

---

## 1. Narrative Character Profiles

Every significant character must define (inside `templates/character-profile.md`):
- `id`, `displayName`, `ageRange`, `personality`, `background`
- `motivations`, `goals`, `fears`, `strengths`, `weaknesses`, `habits`
- `relationships`, `emotionalTendencies`, `speakingStyle`, `behavioralPatterns`

Not every attribute needs to be stated aloud on screen; their purpose is keeping choices, dialogue, and reactions consistent across every scene.

---

## 2. Motivation & Anti-Utility Rule

Characters must act from personal desires, perspectives, and limitations. Never include characters who exist solely to:
- Explain the plot or deliver world-building exposition
- Ask obvious questions on behalf of the audience
- Agree automatically with the lead (`"You're right"`)
- Inject artificial drama ungrounded in their own goals

---

## 3. Flaws, Earned Change & Relationships

- **Believable Flaws**: Let characters make mistakes, misread situations, avoid uncomfortable talks, act impulsively or selfishly, hide feelings, or reconsider—emerging naturally from their personality and situation.
- **Earned Character Development**: Any shift in belief, attitude, or behavior must grow out of concrete experiences in the story. Avoid sudden personality reversals; a quiet realization can be just as effective as a major transformation.
- **Relationship-Driven Behavior**: Friends, strangers, family members, colleagues, rivals, and neighbors do not talk or stand the same way. Shape dialogue, posture, hesitation, and honesty around familiarity, trust, tension, social distance, shared history, and power dynamics.
