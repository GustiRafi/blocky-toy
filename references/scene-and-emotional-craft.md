# Scene Construction & Emotional Storytelling

Load this file at **Stage 1** when breaking a story into scenes (`templates/screenplay.md`) and balancing visual vs. emotional beats.

---

## 1. Scene Purpose & Structure

Every scene must earn its place by doing at least one concrete job: advancing plot, developing a character, shaping a relationship, revealing crucial info, shifting tension, creating an emotional beat, grounding the world, or setting up a later payoff. Cut or merge scenes that contribute nothing new.

Define for every scene:
- `sceneId`, `location`, `time`, `characters`
- `initialSituation`, `sceneObjective`, `conflictOrTension`
- `keyActions`, `dialogue`, `emotionalProgression`, `outcome`, `nextSceneConnection`

---

## 2. Scene Variety & Visual Storytelling

- **Break Repetitive Formulas**: Do not open every scene with a character walking in, talking, and walking out. Vary openings and endings with ongoing activities, environmental interactions, silent discoveries, or mid-action beats.
- **Show Before Telling**: Blocky Toy is a visual animation medium. Communicate key facts, tensions, and shifts through facial expressions, body language, prop placement, environmental details, visual contrast, and physical reactions before leaning on dialogue.
- **Scene Transitions**: Follow narrative logic with straightforward **`cut`** transitions by default (`references/cinematography.md`); never force dramatic transitions between every scene.

---

## 3. Emotional Authenticity, Restraint & Payoff

- **Earned Emotion Only**: Let emotion grow from character history, recurring details, relationships, meaningful objects, visual callbacks, and unresolved choices. Never force emotion via melodramatic speeches, heavy music swells, unnecessary close-ups, exaggerated reactions, or contrived tragedy.
- **Emotional Restraint**: Quiet actions, hesitation, silence, or understated gestures often carry more weight than speeches. Give the audience room to read between the lines.
- **Satisfying Endings**: Choose an ending style suited to the story (`emotional_resolution`, `bittersweet`, `unexpected_revelation`, `humorous_payoff`, `quiet_realization`, `open_ended`, or `satisfying_resolution`). Connect the payoff directly to the premise, motivations, and setups planted earlier—never tack on an unmotivated twist just for shock value.
