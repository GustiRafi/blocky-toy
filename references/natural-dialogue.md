# Natural Dialogue Writing

Load this file at **Stage 1** (Dialogue Writer pass) and before finalizing `templates/dialogue-timeline.md`. Use `references/dialogue-and-acting.md` for technical lip-sync, gaze, and gesture execution.

---

## 1. Pre-Line Context Gate

Before writing any spoken line, verify:
1. Who is speaking, and to whom?
2. What is their relationship and current trust/tension?
3. What does the speaker want from this exchange?
4. What are they feeling right now?
5. What happened immediately before this line?
6. What shifts after this exchange?

Every conversation must advance characterization, relationship, or conflict—not merely fill silence.

---

## 2. Human Conversational Behavior & Rhythm

Real speech is imperfect and responsive. Use these techniques **selectively** (never cramming every line with filler):
- Trailing off or restarting a thought mid-sentence
- Changing mind while speaking, giving brief answers, or asking natural follow-ups
- Polite or accidental interruptions (`interruptedAtSec` in `references/dialogue-and-acting.md`)
- Hesitation, dodging direct questions, or responding with unexpected yet relevant remarks
- Callbacks to earlier conversations, informal phrasing, and leaving certain feelings unsaid
- **Conversational Rhythm**: Mix short exchanges, longer explanations when earned, questions, reactions, silence, and nonverbal beats. Never give every speaker equal word counts; let talkative and terse characters contrast naturally.

---

## 3. Individual Dialogue Identity & Subtext

- **Distinct Voices**: Differentiate every character by vocabulary, sentence length, formality, speaking speed, directness, humor, emotional openness, and fitting regional/cultural phrasing—never letting all cast members sound like the same writer.
- **Subtext**: Characters do not always say what they mean directly. They may change the subject, joke to mask discomfort, answer partially, voice worry indirectly, or stay quiet when unsure what to say. Use subtext where it deepens emotion without turning every line into a riddle.

---

## 4. Anti-AI Dialogue Patterns (Strictly Avoid)

Reject these telltale artificial patterns:
- **Over-Explanatory Dialogue**: Characters explaining facts both participants already know.
- **Unnaturally Polished Essays**: Every line delivered as a complete, textbook-grammatical paragraph.
- **Repetitive Q&A Ping-Pong**: Every turn mirroring the same question-then-answer cadence.
- **Constant Philosophical Quotes**: Ordinary characters speaking in aphorisms or motivational speeches.
- **Artificial Emotional Swings**: Exaggerated shock, tears, or fury unmotivated by the scene's actual stakes.
- **Generic Agreement Fillers**: Avoid stock AI phrasing like *"You're absolutely right."*, *"I completely understand."*, or *"That's a very interesting perspective."*
- **Verbal Exposition Dumps**: Replace "telling the audience the plot" with visual storytelling (`references/scene-and-emotional-craft.md`).
