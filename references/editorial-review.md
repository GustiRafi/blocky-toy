# Editorial Review & Story Quality Control

Load this file at **Stage 1 (Story Editor gate)** after drafting the screenplay (`templates/screenplay.md`) and **before** any 3D scene/audio production begins.

> **Editorial Discipline**: Maximum 4-step writing loop—**(1) Initial screenplay -> (2) One critical Editorial Review -> (3) One targeted revision (only affected sections) -> (4) Production-ready screenplay**. Never rewrite the entire script repeatedly in circles.

---

## 1. Five-Category Review Checklist

Evaluate the draft critically across every category—never auto-approve:

### A. Story Review
- Is the premise genuinely compelling (not just a catchy title)?
- Does the opening establish curiosity or connection fit for the genre?
- Does each beat move the story forward with understandable conflicts and proportionate stakes?
- Are events connected by clear cause and effect (no unexplained knowledge or convenient coincidences)?
- Does the ending feel earned by earlier setups?

### B. Character Review
- Are motivations clear and personal (zero plot-explainer or yes-man characters)?
- Are personalities, flaws, and decisions distinct and consistent?
- Do relationships visibly shape how characters talk and react?
- Is any character change earned rather than abrupt?

### C. Dialogue Review
- Does every line sound natural when spoken aloud?
- Does each character have an unmistakable speaking voice?
- Are sentence lengths and rhythms appropriate to the situation?
- Is unnecessary exposition, repetitive Q&A, generic AI agreement, or forced philosophy removed?
- Does subtext or silence carry weight where direct speech would feel flat?

### D. Scene Review
- Does every scene have a clear purpose and contribute to the next?
- Is scene structure varied (avoiding constant enter -> talk -> exit repetition)?
- Is key story information shown visually where possible rather than explained verbally?
- Does pacing fit the target duration without filler or rushed beats?

### E. Emotional Review
- Are emotional beats built up properly and delivered with authentic restraint?
- Is tone consistent with the declared genre?
- Are character reactions believable for the actual stakes?
- Does the final beat land with the right emotional payoff?

---

## 2. Targeted Revision Rules

- **Idea Mode**: List specific weaknesses found, revise **only** the affected scenes/lines, and lock the screenplay as Production Source of Truth.
- **Script Mode**: Fix visual staging, acting cues, or optional dialogue polish **without** altering the author's main plot, motivations, key events, scene order, relationships, or ending. If a major structural change is warranted, output it separately as an explicit recommendation for the user.
