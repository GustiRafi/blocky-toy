# Story Structure, Genre & Duration Pacing

Load this file during **Stage 1** when developing a premise, plot arc, genre tone, and duration budget.

---

## 1. Premise Development & Hooks

Before outlining scenes, establish a premise with genuine narrative legs—not just a catchy title:
- **6 Premise Questions**: (1) What is the story about? (2) Who is the central character? (3) What do they want? (4) What prevents them from getting it? (5) Why should the audience care? (6) What makes this story specifically interesting?
- **Sustainable Scope**: Ensure the premise carries enough material for the requested runtime without padding simple ideas into empty filler scenes.
- **Story Hooks**: Open with a clear reason to keep watching—an unusual situation, intriguing question, unexpected event, vivid character introduction, interesting visual situation, emotional moment, or relatable everyday problem. Match the hook to the genre; never force a mystery or dramatic twist into every story.

---

## 2. Narrative Progression, Conflict & Stakes

- **Progressive Change**: Every sequence must move forward—revealing new information, forcing a decision, complicating a conflict, shifting a relationship, raising stakes, deepening a misunderstanding, or giving an earlier event new meaning. Never repeat the same information across scenes.
- **5 Conflict Dimensions**:
  - *Internal*: fear, guilt, uncertainty, ambition, regret.
  - *Interpersonal*: disagreements, misunderstandings, competing desires.
  - *Situational*: unexpected circumstances, limited resources, tough choices.
  - *Environmental*: weather, location, physical obstacles, external conditions.
  - *Social*: expectations, responsibilities, community or family pressures.
  - Not every story needs a villain or physical danger; quiet everyday conflicts work powerfully when the audience understands why they matter to the characters.
- **Proportionate Stakes**: Put something meaningful at risk—relationships, trust, reputation, identity, opportunities, belonging, memories, or emotional security—scaled naturally to the story without melodramatic exaggeration.
- **Cause & Effect**: Actions flow from believable motivations; decisions carry consequences; discoveries have grounded causes; character knowledge stays strictly consistent with what they experienced. Avoid plot-convenient events or repeated coincidences solving major conflicts.

---

## 3. Genre & Tone Awareness

Establish upfront: **Primary genre** (and optional secondary), **intended tone**, **emotional direction**, **target audience**, **narrative intensity**, and **desired ending style**.

Supported genres include: `comedy`, `drama`, `mystery`, `thriller`, `adventure`, `slice_of_life`, `fantasy`, `sci_fi`, `horror`, `family_animation`, `coming_of_age`, `romance`, and `documentary_style`.
- Treat genre as a compass, not a rigid formula: a `mystery` should not automatically become `horror`; a `comedy` should not rely on random gags; a `drama` does not require nonstop sadness; `slice_of_life` still needs small meaningful observations, interactions, or shifts.

---

## 4. Duration & Story Pacing

Adapt narrative density cleanly to target runtime (`references/long-form-production.md`):
- **Short-Form (`15–60s`)**: Establish the situation immediately, center on one crisp narrative idea, skip slow warmups, and land a clear payoff.
- **Short Films (`1–10 min`)**: Develop a grounded premise, give relationships room to breathe, let conflicts build across scenes, and avoid filler padding.
- **Longer Productions (`> 10 min`)**: Structure multi-stage arcs, maintain strict character/world continuity, and add subplots only when they deepen the main story or character arc.
- Treat duration targets as flexible pacing bands—never invent meaningless scenes just to fill seconds.
